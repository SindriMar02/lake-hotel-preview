THE HOUSE

Egilsstaðir 1, on the bank of Lagarfljót.

Since1884

Jón Bergsson and Margrét Pétursdóttir bought the farm in 1889. Their descendants live in six houses on the land today, and in 1997 the hotel came back to the family.

Built inCONCRETE

The oldest part of the house went up in 1903 and 1904: two storeys and a basement, among the first houses in Fljótsdalshérað built of concrete. An extension finished in 1920 almost doubled it.

Through this houseTHE FIRST CALL

The house also held Jón Bergsson's store, the post office and the telephone exchange, which ran until 1952. Iceland's first long-distance call, over the line from Seyðisfjörður, went through it.

The restaurantELDHÚSIÐ

MOSTLY ICELANDIC, OFTEN FROM THE FARMS NEARBY. THE THREE-COURSE FARM FOOD DIRECT DINNER IS THE KITCHEN'S PRIDE.

A hot pool at 40°C, a mild sauna at about 65°C and a cold tub at 10°C, with a view over Lagarfljót. Open every day from 10:00 to 22:00. Hotel guests pay ISK 2,000.

On the restaurant wallsTHE OLD WINDOWS

WHEN NEW WINDOWS WENT IN, THE OLD SINGLE-GLAZED ONES WERE KEPT. SOME NOW HANG ON THE RESTAURANT WALLS, BESIDE PHOTOGRAPHS AND PAINTINGS OF THE PLACE AND ITS PEOPLE.

Notes on the house

GUNNLAUGUR IS THE SON OF MARGRÉT PÉTURSDÓTTIR, A GRANDDAUGHTER OF THE JÓN AND MARGRÉT WHO BOUGHT THE FARM IN 1889.

SVEINN AND SIGRÍÐUR FANNEY RAN THE GUESTHOUSE FOR HALF A CENTURY FROM 1920. THEIR DAUGHTER ÁSDÍS RAN IT FOR EIGHTEEN YEARS FROM 1970.

AROUND 1910 SIGRÍÐUR JÓNSDÓTTIR LAID OUT AN ORNAMENTAL GARDEN HERE, A NOVELTY IN THE COUNTRYSIDE. AROUND 1930 SVEINN PLANTED TREES IN THE RUINS OF THE OLD TURF FARMHOUSE.
